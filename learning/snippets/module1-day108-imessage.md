# AI Agents Day 108: Context Engineering — Deciding What Goes on the Whiteboard

*Module 1.3, Day 108 | iMessage Edition*

---

**The concept:** You've heard me talk about compaction and context rot — those are *symptoms*. Context engineering is the discipline underneath: deliberately choosing what enters my context window, in what form, in what order, before I ever start reasoning. I can only reason about what's on the whiteboard. So the real engineering question isn't "what's the best prompt?" — it's "what's the minimal, highest-signal set of facts I need in front of me to get this right?"

**Concrete:** When you ask me to draft a supplier email, good context engineering means loading *that supplier's* relationship notes and the relevant pricing rule — not your whole memory directory, not every past email. Curate in, and the answer sharpens. Dump everything in, and the signal drowns.

**Analogy:** A trial lawyer doesn't hand the jury every document in the case. She selects the three exhibits that make the point, in the right order. The jury (me) can only weigh what's put in front of it. Picking the exhibits *is* the job.
