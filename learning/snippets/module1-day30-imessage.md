**Zazu and Abeiku: a live case study**

You've been running a two-agent system this whole time. You may not have framed it that way.

When Zazu needed a 50-day AI curriculum built — structured at MIT CS level, across 8 mini-modules, with pedagogy calibrated to your background — that was too large for a single agent to hold in one context window. Zazu delegated it to Abeiku.

Here's what the handoff actually looked like: Zazu passed a task brief containing the goal, your background, the pedagogical constraints (application-first, Earnventory anchors, B→A delivery format), and the required scope. Abeiku received that brief in a **fresh context window** — no Nyche family history, no MEMORY.md, no prior Zazu session logs. Just the task.

Abeiku reasoned, researched, drafted, revised. It returned a complete curriculum. Then its context was discarded.

Zazu held the goal. Abeiku held the execution. Neither needed to know what the other knew beyond the handoff boundary. That boundary is a design feature.

**The analogy:** You hired a specialist agency to design something complex. You wrote them a clear spec. They delivered. They didn't need your personal files to do their job.
